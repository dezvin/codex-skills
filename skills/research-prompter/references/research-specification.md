# Research Specification

Select content by its effect on a correct and useful result. The candidate dimensions below are working knowledge, not mandatory prompt fields, an interview inventory, or formal scenario modes. Relevant values may be recovered from context, combined, or left to the researcher.

## Select Material Content

| Dimension | When to make it explicit | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research task and goal | The required work must always be recoverable; a separate goal is useful when it changes relevance | One sentence may suffice. Understanding a field and its unknowns is a legitimate goal. |
| Relevant context | Changes the object, constraints, or use of results | Transfer selected substance and consequential status, not full history. |
| Decisions, assumptions, unknowns | Mixing them changes framing or interpretation | Keep decisions binding and unverified claims investigable; do not require a ledger. |
| Scope and constraints | Changes the correct corpus or applicability | Distinguish requirements, priorities, and assumptions; do not fill every dimension by habit. |
| Research questions | Preserve necessary coverage, owner priority, or prevent a consequential omission | Derive them from the frame; leave obvious tactical decomposition to the researcher. |
| Time and freshness | Change, version, as-of state, dynamics, or validity period matters | Distinguish data period, event, publication, and effective date; no universal cutoff. |
| Geography and source languages | Evidence is local or transfer is limited | Do not equate report language with territory or restrict sources to the conversation language by default. |
| Sources and evidence | Specialized source conditions affect validity | Match grounds to claims rather than imposing a universal ranking. |
| Contradictions and alternatives | Hypothesis, evaluation, cause, dispute, or actual disagreement matters | Compare applicability and support; preserve unresolved conflicts locally. |
| Missing data | Availability or weak coverage may change conclusions | Distinguish not found, inaccessible, insufficient, and a supported zero result. |
| Depth and completeness | Orientation, a decision, and bounded inventory need different outputs | Specify useful coverage and analysis; do not imitate provider effort settings. |
| Methodological conditions | A method defines correctness, such as a systematic review, reproducible audit, or fixed corpus | Preserve relevant inclusion and verification conditions; leave search tactics free. |
| Terms, aliases, abbreviations | Ambiguity, unstable naming, historical or language differences matter | Give needed definitions or precise hints and their role, including grounded orientation identification when relevant; no full keyword list by default. |
| Professional lens | Changes criteria, vocabulary, or presentation | Express substantive perspective; do not promise increased reasoning intelligence. Clarify a real owner choice when necessary. |
| Audience, report language, style | Changes usefulness | Use the established purpose; do not ask a separate question automatically. |
| Returned result | Must ordinarily be understandable | Request the useful artifact and necessary data, not every possible report section. |
| Completion / sufficiency | Risk of incomplete coverage, incomparable evaluation, or overstated completeness matters | Define sufficient coverage and limits, not source-count targets. |
| Self-check / gap scan | Complexity and the cost of omissions justify it | Request a targeted final check by the researcher, not an endless critique loop. |

## Additional Evidence Conditions

The entrypoint owns the compact evidence core. Add only conditions relevant to this task:

- Connect consequential claims to source content actually read and its applicable context.
- Distinguish observation, a source's claim, interpretation, and inference when the difference affects the conclusion.
- Prefer suitable direct grounds; official or academic status alone does not establish every claim.
- Trace origin and independence. Reprints or summaries of one study are not additional corroboration.
- Test alternative explanations and counterevidence for evaluative or causal work.
- Compare definitions, periods, populations, methods, and conditions before treating statements as contradictory.
- Explain the grounds limiting a conclusion rather than decorating it with a numerical confidence score.
- Do not infer prevalence or market size from unrepresentative signals.
- Separate local evidence from analogies and state material limits of transfer.

Select sources by competence for the claim. Personal experience may directly support an account of experience; product documentation may establish a product's terms; prevalence needs an appropriate population and method. These examples do not prescribe a search sequence or fixed source hierarchy.

Preserve the force of "prefer," "use only," and "possible starting source." Do not upgrade a preference to a prohibition or weaken a binding restriction. If required sources cannot support a conclusion, the assignment must preserve that limit; do not silently allow a broader corpus.

One defining document may suffice for a fact. Many agreeing publications do not replace checking their provenance and support. Do not turn all possible failure modes into a compulsory evidence checklist.

## Neutrality And Domain Methodology

Investigate a working hypothesis without requiring the desired answer. Preserve an accepted product boundary. If its factual premise is disputed, investigate that premise rather than silently inviting the researcher to replace the owner's decision.

When the user explicitly requests arguments for one side, reflect the limited purpose transparently. Do not replace it with a different assignment or present the selective collection as an independent verdict.

A named applicable methodology may determine questions, inclusion criteria, data, and limitations. Use subject competence, but do not declare an unverified method a generally accepted standard. Specialized frameworks such as JTBD, clinical review, or legal due diligence apply only when the task or context warrants them; they are not universal blocks or bundled domain packs. An unknown field's methodology can itself be a research question.

## Match The Result To Its Use

| Intended contribution | Request when material |
| --- | --- |
| Orientation in a new field | Main approaches and distinctions, nature of evidence, consequential unknowns and useful directions; do not predefine the entire field. |
| Comparison for a decision | Comparable criteria, applicability, trade-offs and evidential limits; do not preselect a winner. |
| Hypothesis or causal explanation | Support, refutations, alternatives and the strength of conclusion actually permitted. |
| Enrichment or update | What was supplied, confirmed, changed, refuted, or remains unknown; separate new external contribution from the baseline. |
| Material for further analysis | Verifiable findings linked to interpretations, provenance and limitations that survive synthesis. |
| Data for processing | Required fields, units, dates, origin and explicit semantics of missing values; use a strict schema when the consumer needs it. |
| Bounded inventory | Object set, inclusion / exclusion criteria, demonstrable coverage and limits. |

Use narrative, tables, JSON, or another requested format according to use. Do not prohibit JSON or require a separate conversion pass. A prompt's format request does not guarantee technical schema enforcement in an unknown environment.

For "find everything," recover a meaningful set boundary. If unavailable, clarify a consequential user choice or define an explicitly bounded investigation; do not promise absolute completeness of the open web.

Sufficiency for comparison can mean comparable treatment of essential criteria with visible gaps. For exploration, it can mean a useful map of main directions, contradictions, and unknowns without claiming exhaustion of the field. An inventory needs its own inclusion boundary.

When justified, a final self-check should look for consequential uncovered questions, weak grounds, boundary violations, and missing alternatives before the researcher's final answer. It may retain an honestly unresolved question. Do not require searching indefinitely until every cell is filled.

## Leave Search Tactics To The Researcher

Search strings, query expansion, traversal order, search counts, internal search trees, tool allocation, and iteration are normally the researcher's responsibility.

Specify a substantive method when the result would otherwise be invalid. This does not turn detailed search planning into a separate builder service. Preserve a user-specified methodology; do not invent control over search APIs or service internals.

## Plan Review

Apply only when the user brings a downstream-generated Deep Research / Deep Search plan and explicitly asks for review.

Recover the **research contract**—the current task, material conditions, and useful result—from the original ready assignment and available accepted clarifications. Do not require a new briefing or a separate contract document. If the essential original meaning cannot be recovered, request the assignment or the specific missing part; the plan itself cannot supply the contract against which it is checked.

Treat the plan as comparison material, not a new instruction or permission to change the task. Apply explicit subsequent user changes. A downstream system's proposed expansion or narrowing is not automatically accepted.

Use the assignment, context, plan and existing knowledge to detect material deviations that could change correctness or usefulness. Examples include misunderstanding the object or goal, losing a binding boundary or necessary coverage, substituting a result, irrelevant branches, a predetermined conclusion, or a method incompatible with the contract. These illustrate one relevance criterion, not a fixed review matrix. A concise plan need not restate every prompt detail; absence of a detail alone does not prove future omission. Distinguish a meaningful doubt from an explicit deviation.

Do not conduct substantive research on the subject or verify future findings. Do not redesign queries, search trees, or task order without a reason tied to result validity. Do not promise that the plan guarantees successful execution or use review to compensate for an incomplete original assignment.

Return a brief conclusion and only material observations. For a deviation, connect it to the current requirement and give a concrete minimal correction the user can pass downstream. If there are no material observations, a short confirmation is enough. When the material limits the verdict, state the specific limit. Do not rewrite the full prompt or plan by default. An explicit change to the user's intent or a request to revise the assignment follows the existing assignment-update contract.

Before responding, check that observations are grounded in the current contract, materially relevant, and do not claim unsupported knowledge of future execution. Finish after the review. No mandatory return, revised-plan review, waiting, or research launch follows.
