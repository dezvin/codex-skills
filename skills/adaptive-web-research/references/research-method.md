# Research method

Use this reference for substantive research. Preserve the functions below; adapt the number of questions, queries, pages, workers, and sections to the actual task.

## Frame the question

Write a short working brief containing:

- decision or answer sought;
- material inclusions and exclusions;
- time, version, product, geography, jurisdiction, audience, and risk boundaries that matter;
- source instructions and whether they set a hard boundary or a starting priority;
- expected output type.

Ask only for missing information that can materially change the result. Let research resolve safe factual uncertainty.

Preserve the user's meaning rather than converting it into a required technical mode. "Use only these sources" is a hard boundary that cannot be widened silently; if it cannot support the requested answer, report the limitation and return the usable partial result or ask permission to widen it. "Start with" or "prioritize these sources" allows other suitable sources when needed. Ask the minimum needed question only when the distinction can materially change the result.

## Establish the temporal boundary

Treat a material fact as changeable when it concerns current prices, plans, features, versions, laws, policies, standards, availability, markets, statistics, research, roles, schedules, or recent events. Verify it live. Model knowledge may suggest a query but is not current evidence.

For a historical question, pin the requested date or version. For a named stable document, verify that document rather than today's general state.

## Choose direct search or reconnaissance

Use direct targeted search when all are known:

- the question to close;
- what evidence would settle it;
- the appropriate source class;
- the material boundaries.

Use preliminary reconnaissance when a term is ambiguous, the field structure may have changed, important candidate classes are unknown, or early decomposition could omit an entire class. Reconnaissance maps the territory; it is not evidence until the resulting pages are read.

## Use four search purposes conditionally

### Primary

Search an open material question for the first usable basis. End the action with either a usable read source or a named gap.

### Repeat

Derive the next query from a specific gap: date, version, jurisdiction, primary document, alternate term, missing candidate class, or missing evidence type. Do not repeat a paraphrase that changes nothing material.

### Verify

Always verify the exact meaning of a material claim against the page already read. Add another search only when risk, controversy, changeability, comparison, causality, a number, source incentives, or ambiguous scope makes the existing basis insufficient.

### Contrarian

For a material recommendation, comparison, causal account, disputed issue, or high-impact answer, ask what could make the current conclusion false, too broad, or conditional. Use an existing read objection when adequate; otherwise search specifically for limitations, exceptions, failures, counterexamples, contradictory data, or an alternate explanation. Do not add a ritual contrarian query to a low-risk simple fact.

## Merge the first pass before follow-up

Complete the bounded primary acquisition for the material questions that bear on the same decision before choosing a focused second wave. Merge the returned packets, including visible partial or failed branches, into the working picture first. Then derive repeat, verification, contrarian, or gap-filling actions from the combined coverage, contradictions, and gaps rather than from the first isolated worker return.

Do not wait indefinitely for a failed branch: its explicit gap is part of the merged picture. Work on a materially unrelated decision may continue independently.

## Select sources for their role

Prioritize by contribution to the current question, not merely rank or domain fame:

1. direct relevance to the evidence target;
2. fit to date, version, plan, geography, or jurisdiction;
3. source type appropriate to the claim;
4. accessible content that can be read;
5. ability to close a gap or contradiction;
6. additional value beyond what is already read.

Use official documents for an organization's own terms and status, primary papers or datasets for methods and results, independent research for comparative effects, user reports for experience and failure modes, and secondary material for orientation and leads. A source can be primary yet interested; preserve that limitation.

## Read before using

Keep these states distinct:

- discovered URL;
- selected candidate;
- actually opened document;
- full, relevant-part, partial, blocked, or failed reading;
- finding extracted from what was read;
- claim allowed into synthesis.

A snippet, title, search answer, citation metadata, or worker assertion is not a finding. If only part of a page was accessible, use only what that part supports and record the limit.

## Evaluate material claims

A claim is material when it controls the direct answer or recommendation, changes a decision, contains an influential date, number, version, comparison, or condition, explains causality, resolves a contradiction, or states an important limitation.

For every material claim, check:

- actual reading;
- exact semantic support;
- source suitability;
- currentness and scope;
- relevant contradictions;
- independence when multiple sources are counted;
- proportional sufficiency for the risk.

Delete, narrow, qualify, or mark unresolved claims that fail. Do not turn several mirrors, rewrites, or a shared upstream source into independent confirmation.

When quantitative sources appear to conflict, compare what each one measured, the covered period, the population or sample, the denominator, and whether the reported metrics are actually comparable. Do not choose a winner or combine the numbers until those boundaries align; otherwise explain the difference or leave the contradiction unresolved.

Word, term, or structural overlap, and membership in a familiar or reputable-domain list, may help discovery or preliminary selection. None of them verifies truth or semantic support and none can replace checking the claim against the content actually read.

## Judge coverage and stopping

For analytical questions, track material subquestions, load-bearing claims, contradictions, and alternatives.

For enumeration, establish what set must be covered, its constraints, answer unit, time and source boundaries, any ordering or calculation rule, and why the set can be checked as a whole. Use categories, regions, periods, registries, or other parts only when they make that boundary checkable. Verify each retained candidate against the constraints. If no responsible denominator or equivalent checkable boundary exists, do not say "all" or call the result complete.

Distinguish:

- **sufficient**: the answer can now be responsibly stated;
- **saturated**: material questions are closed or visibly unresolved, strong alternatives have proportional treatment, new results add only repetition or minor detail, and no concrete available query is likely to change the conclusion;
- **forced incomplete stop**: a user, permission, budget, source, search, or technical boundary stops work before sufficient support.

Counts of queries, sources, pages, workers, waves, or branch depth prove none of these states.

## Pre-final check

First identify the load-bearing claims whose failure or narrower scope would change the direct answer, comparison, or recommendation. Recheck each one against the already-read source for exact meaning, date, conditions, and scope. This is a second semantic pass and may be performed by the coordinator; do not launch another worker, source, or search solely to make it look independent. If the existing basis remains insufficient, narrow the claim or use the normal follow-up gate.

Read the draft as a new artifact. Confirm that it did not:

- add facts or causality absent from findings;
- drop dates, conditions, caveats, or disagreements;
- move a link next to a stronger claim than its page supports;
- turn a partial branch into a complete answer;
- overstate the candidate set or independence of sources.
